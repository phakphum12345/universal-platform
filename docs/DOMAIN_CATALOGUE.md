# Universal Platform Domain Catalogue

**Status:** Extensible taxonomy proposal. This catalogue is intentionally broad and is not a closed list.

## 1. Formal and foundational disciplines
Mathematics; logic; set theory; statistics; probability; optimization; information theory; systems theory; operations research; computational methods; geometry; numerical analysis.

## 2. Computing and digital systems
Computer science; software engineering; programming languages; algorithms; data structures; databases; distributed systems; operating systems; compilers; networks; cybersecurity; cloud and edge computing; AI and machine learning; human-computer interaction; graphics; robotics software; developer tooling.

## 3. Hardware, electronics, and physical engineering
Computer hardware; ICs and semiconductors; digital/analog/mixed-signal electronics; PCB and board design; sensors and modules; embedded systems (analysis/simulation initially); electrical and power engineering; telecommunications; RF; signal processing; control theory (simulation initially); mechanical, civil, chemical, aerospace, automotive, materials, industrial, manufacturing, energy, robotics and mechatronics engineering.

## 4. Physics and natural sciences
Classical mechanics; electromagnetism; thermodynamics; statistical physics; optics; acoustics; fluid mechanics; quantum physics where validated; chemistry; materials science; astronomy; earth science; geology; meteorology; oceanography; environmental science.

## 5. Life sciences
Biology; microbiology; genetics; neuroscience; ecology; biomedical and health sciences (with appropriate professional and safety boundaries).

## 6. Human, social, and applied sciences
Economics; finance; accounting; business; management; law and policy research; education; linguistics; psychology; sociology; anthropology; geography; design; architecture; agriculture; food science; logistics; public health; safety and quality.

## 7. Interdisciplinary and emerging domains
Bioinformatics; computational science; computational engineering; cyber-physical systems; digital twins; climate technology; quantum information; human-centered AI; smart manufacturing; geospatial intelligence; sustainability; and future fields added through review.

## 8. Cross-disciplinary engineering applications
3D CAD and parametric design; machine design; mechanisms and kinematics; structural/civil design; thermal and fluid systems; electrical/electronic equipment; tool and fixture generation; manufacturing process planning; tolerance engineering; materials selection; simulation and verification.

## 9. Domain module contract
Each module must define: domain scope; supported questions/tasks; authoritative sources and standards; terminology and ontology; input/output schemas; calculation or simulation methods; assumptions and uncertainty; safety and regulatory constraints; test/reference cases; evidence and citations; required expertise/review; cost and compute needs; supported platforms; and maturity level.

## 10. Catalogue governance
New domains can be proposed without changing the core. Avoid claiming universal expertise: each domain has a maturity level and explicit limitations. High-impact domains require qualified review and authoritative standards. UEIC coordinates modules but does not erase disciplinary boundaries or replace qualified experts.
